Three directions for the page

Same palette, same fonts, same radii — your real tokens from tailwind.config.js. What differs is the shape of the page and how hard it tries. Your fail condition is "reads as AI slop", so the question is really: which of these would you not be embarrassed to put on your GitHub profile? Click one (or two) — and tell me in the terminal what specifically feels off.

Direction A

Plain-spoken

No hero theatre. The page opens the way a good README opens: a sentence that states the problem, a sentence that states the answer, two links. The persuasion is the specificity below the fold, not the headline. Anti-slop by subtraction — there is nothing to pattern-match against because there is almost nothing there.

Direction B

Product shot leads

The conventional shape: centred headline, two buttons, then a large screenshot of the real dashboard bleeding off the bottom edge. The screenshot does the arguing, so the copy can stay short. Honest, because it's your actual UI — but this is the silhouette every AI-generated landing page has, so it lives or dies on the screenshot being good.

Direction C

The data is the hero

The hero's visual isn't decoration, it's the product's actual output: a real latency trace across the full width, with one real outage in it, headline sitting over the quiet part. Then the three-tier storage model drawn as a diagram rather than claimed in a sentence. You could not paste this hero onto another product — which is the one thing a generated page never manages.
